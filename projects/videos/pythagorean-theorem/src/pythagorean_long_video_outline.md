# Pythagorean Theorem — Proofs, Consequences, and Uses

Format: horizontal 1920×1080, 30 FPS, approximately 12 minutes.

## 0:00–0:35 — Hook and statement

- A glowing right triangle appears, then squares grow on all three sides.
- Narration: “For a right triangle with legs `a` and `b`, and hypotenuse `c`, the areas satisfy `a² + b² = c²`.”
- Visual: a 3–4–5 triangle becomes three labeled squares.

## 0:35–1:50 — Proof 1: four-triangle dissection

- Four congruent right triangles assemble inside a square of side `a + b`.
- The remaining central region is a square with area `c²`.
- Area comparison:
  - outer area: `(a + b)²`
  - pieces: `4 × (ab/2) + c²`
  - simplify to `a² + b² = c²`.

## 1:50–3:10 — Proof 2: rearrangement/scissors proof

- The same four triangles slide smoothly into a second arrangement.
- In the first arrangement, the remaining region is `c²`; in the second, it is the two regions `a²` and `b²`.
- The triangles are unchanged, so the remaining areas are equal.

## 3:10–4:35 — Proof 3: Euclid’s proof

- Draw squares on all three sides of the right triangle.
- Add altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse.
- Animate Euclid’s two corresponding rectangles, each equal in area to one leg square by shared base/altitude and congruent triangles.
- Combine the rectangles to obtain the hypotenuse square.

## 4:35–5:25 — Proof 4: scaling/similarity

- Drop the altitude to form two smaller triangles similar to the original.
- Show `a² = c·p` and `b² = c·q`, where `p + q = c`.
- Add: `a² + b² = c(p + q) = c²`.

## 5:25–6:10 — Proof 5: area-preserving shearing

- Shear rectangular regions without changing their bases and heights.
- Transform the two leg-square areas into matching pieces of the hypotenuse square.
- Caption: “A shear preserves area.”

## 6:10–6:55 — Proof 6: differential viewpoint

- Let a right triangle change infinitesimally.
- Use `c² = a² + b²`; differentiate to show `c dc = a da + b db`.
- Animate tiny edge changes and area strips.

## 6:55–7:50 — Pythagorean triples and converse

- Generate 3–4–5, 5–12–13, and 8–15–17 triangles.
- Show Euclid’s generator: `m² − n²`, `2mn`, `m² + n²`.
- Converse: if positive sides satisfy `a² + b² = c²`, the angle opposite `c` is a right angle.

## 7:50–8:35 — Incommensurable lengths

- Build the square-root spiral starting with unit segments.
- Highlight the first hypotenuse: `√2`.
- Narration: “Not every geometric length is a rational multiple of the unit.”

## 8:35–9:25 — Complex numbers and Euclidean distance

- Complex plane: `z = x + iy`; draw the right triangle to the point `(x, y)`.
- Show `|z|² = x² + y²`.
- Transform into the distance formula `d² = (Δx)² + (Δy)²`.

## 9:25–10:10 — Trigonometric identity

- Unit circle and a right triangle.
- Divide `a² + b² = c²` by `c²`.
- On the unit circle, reveal `sin²θ + cos²θ = 1`.

## 10:10–10:55 — Vectors and cross product

- Two perpendicular 3D vectors form a right triangle.
- Show `||u + v||² = ||u||² + ||v||²` when `u·v = 0`.
- Reveal the cross-product magnitude `||u×v|| = ||u|| ||v|| sin θ`; at `90°`, it is maximal.

## 10:55–12:00 — Closing synthesis

- Rapid callback montage: dissection, Euclid, spiral, complex plane, unit circle, 3D vectors.
- Closing text: “One right triangle. Hundreds of proofs. A language for distance.”
